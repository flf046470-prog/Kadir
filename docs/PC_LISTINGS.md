# Steam and Epic Games Store listings

The text and art for a **Coming Soon** page on both PC stores: what to paste into Steamworks
(Store Page Admin) and the Epic Developer Portal (Store Presence), and what to upload. The art is
generated by `npm run pack:pc:listing` (after `npm run build:client`); the copy is below, and
`scripts/lib/copy.test.ts` holds every block in this file to its limit and every number it
claims to the game's own catalogue, so the text cannot drift from the game the way Meta's did
(see `scripts/lib/copy.mjs`).

Specs were read from the stores' own documentation on 2026-10-03:
partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/assets (graphical assets, library assets, the capsule rules) and
dev.epicgames.com/docs/epic-games-store (store presence, offer details, product page media,
distribution requirements). What neither store publishes is said to be unpublished rather than
guessed at.

## Before the page goes up: the release blocker

**The build is ready to play online; the server it plays on is not up.** The PC build is an
Electron shell that serves the game from a server bundled into the app on `127.0.0.1`
(`packaging/steam/main.cjs`). That server tells the page to play online on the hosted origin the
build was packed with — `npm run pack:steam -- --online https://<hosted origin>` — and the page
falls back to the bundled server, with an offline menu that says why, when the hosted one cannot be
reached or speaks another protocol. `npm run check:crossplay` puts a PC page and a browser player in
one room on one server, and runs in CI.

What is missing is that server. Railway's trial ended on 2026-09-22, and the domain it sits on
also decides the Android package's permanent identity (`CLAUDE.md`, "Release identity"). Until it
exists, a build has nowhere to be pointed: packed without `--online` it plays alone, and
`pack:steam` says so in yellow. Everything multiplayer in the copy below — 32 players, proximity
voice, friends and parties — reaches other people through that one server and nowhere else.

It also settles Epic's distribution requirement that "products with online multiplayer
functionality must support crossplay across all PC storefronts where the product is distributed":
the Steam build, the Epic build and the browser all play on the same server, so crossplay holds by
construction.

A Coming Soon page can go up before the server does; a release cannot.

## Name

```
Kangaroo Chase
```

The same name as every other store package. No subtitle: Steam allows "the game name and/or
official subtitle" on a capsule and nothing else, and there is no official subtitle.

## Short description

270 of a maximum 300 characters.

```
Hop, climb and wall-bounce as a kangaroo, or any of 16 animals, through jungle, glacier and
outback. Tag, hunt, brawl and play Roo Ball across 10 game modes with up to 32 players and
proximity voice chat. No loot boxes, and nothing for sale makes you faster or stronger.
```

Used on both stores. Steamworks describes its limit only as "a few hundred characters"; the form

so the same 300 keeps one text for both.

## About this game

2031 of a maximum 10,000 characters.

```
Kangaroo Chase is a physical tag game for up to 32 players. You do not press a button to move
like a kangaroo: you build up a hop, lean into it, kick off walls and grab ledges, and the same
rules decide every chase whether you play with a keyboard and mouse or a gamepad.

Pick from 16 animals: a kangaroo, a wolf, a penguin, a shark, a dragon and more. They look
nothing alike and play alike. Every animal is tuned within 3% of a common baseline, and a bigger
animal never gets a bigger hitbox.

GAME MODES

- Kangaroo Chase: the tag mode the game is named after. Escape the chaser through the trees,
the cave and the canyon, or close the gap if you are it.
- The Hunt: one armed hunter against a team of survivors and a clock. Earn cash mid-round for
traps, smoke and armour.
- Roo Ball: two teams, one giant ball and no hands. Hop into it to score.
- Conversion Duel: a fistfight for keeps. Lose, and you become the winner's species.
- Freeze Tag, King of the Hill, Infection, Parkour Race and VR Boxing, plus a Training Room to
learn the moves against bots. That makes 10 game modes, and private rooms take house rules.

THREE WORLDS

A jungle with a river, a cave and a canyon. A glacier of ice towers, bridges and a crevasse. An
outback station of red rock, a gorge and a creek. Every one is ringed by cliffs, so nobody falls
off the edge of the world.

PLAY TOGETHER

Voice chat fades with distance, so you hear the players who are actually near you, and you can
mute, block or report anyone from the pause menu. Add friends, form a party and follow your
leader into a match. Practise offline against bots any time.

FAIR BY DESIGN

No loot boxes. No pay-to-win. Every animal, gadget and game mode is yours from the start.
Season cosmetics are earned by playing, never bought, and the server, not your PC, decides
every round result.

VR

VR support through OpenXR is planned for launch: climb by reaching for a ledge, punch by
throwing a punch. Flat-screen play needs no headset and loses nothing.
```

Steam publishes no limit for this section; Epic's is 10,000 characters for its About section, so
that is the limit held. No links, no other games, no imitation of the store's own interface —
all three are refused by Steam's description rules. No other storefront is named, which keeps the
same text clear of Epic's rule on media that references other platforms.

Every claim was checked against the code, not written to sound right: 16 animals and 10 modes are
`listAnimals()` and `listModes()`; 32 players is `KC_MAX_PLAYERS`; 3% is `FEEL_BAND` and the
hitbox is unchanged by `visual.scale` (`AnimalVisual`); mute/block/report is the Players & safety
menu; party-follow is `SocialService`; earned season cosmetics are `SEASON_SHELF` and the season
track, with `validateCatalog` refusing a money price at boot; the walls are `enclose`'s cliffs.
**VR is said to be planned, not supported**: the PC build hands VR to Chrome or Edge through the
OpenXR runtime because Electron has no WebXR (`packaging/steam/README.md`), and that path has
never run against a real headset. Tick Steam's "VR Supported" box when it has, not before — a
store page that promises VR the launcher cannot deliver is a refund queue.

## Release date

Use **"To be announced"** on Steam and "Estimated" with no date on Epic until the date is real.
Steam requires the Coming Soon page to have been public for at least two weeks before release and
asks for the page to be submitted for review at least seven business days before it goes up, so a
date announced on the page is a date the review calendar has to be able to meet.

## Trailer

`npm run pack:trailer` films the built game and writes
`packaging/steam/listing/trailer/kangaroo-chase-trailer.mp4`, with the same file as
`packaging/epic/listing/carousel/00-trailer.mp4`: 1920x1080, 30 fps, H.264 High, AAC 48 kHz stereo
at 320 kb/s, which is Epic's carousel specification and what Steam recommends. It needs an ffmpeg
with libx264 (`pip install imageio-ffmpeg`), because Playwright's own ffmpeg writes only VP8.

Every shot is solo practice with bots, filmed through the menus a player uses, with the game's
clock stepped exactly 1/30 s per frame. Under swiftshader the game draws about a frame a second,
so recorded in real time it would play back as a slideshow. The interface is hidden, and the
soundtrack is the game's own score rendered offline. The recorded sound effects are left out
because their commercial use depends on the ElevenLabs plan (`assets/audio/provenance.json`).

## Putting the Epic page up, in the portal's own order

Nothing in this repository can do these steps, and that is not a gap a tool closes. The Developer
Portal (dev.epicgames.com/portal) has no API for store pages; it is a dashboard behind the account
holder's own Epic login. Onboarding is a contract (the Distribution Agreement, accepted by a named
person), a payment and identity, tax and payout checks. Publishing makes the page public and
indexable on the web. So every step below is the account holder's, and the numbering is Epic's
own (Get Started steps 1 and 2, read on 2026-10-03).

**1. Onboarding.** Expect up to 10 business days, mostly verification.

- Create the account and organization, then *Your products → Create Product*. The name is
  "Kangaroo Chase".
- Under *Epic Games Store*:
  - accept the Distribution Agreement (required);
  - pay the $100 per-product fee;
  - answer the questionnaire, which is non-binding.
- Complete trader verification, the tax profile (W-9 or W-8BEN) and the payout account.

**2. Product home page ("Coming Soon").** Epic estimates a week for this step, plus a review of
two days or more.

- **A. Store Settings.**
  - *Product configuration*: the name, *Short description* above, and genres and features as in
    *Tags (Steam)* above.
  - *Regions and ratings*: choose the regions. An IARC rating is recommended, not required.
- **B. Offers.** The base product offer already exists, with type *Game*.
  - Fill its required information.
  - Offer images: `offer_landscape.png` (2560x1440) and `offer_portrait.png` (1200x1600).
  - Logo: `logo.png` (960x540, transparent).
  - The price is a decision, not a fact this file can supply (see *Tags*: no "Free to Play" until
    it is made).
- **C. Dates.**
  - Previously released on PC: no.
  - Discoverable: as soon as the offer is pushed to Live.
  - Pre-purchase: no.
  - Launch: **Launch date unknown**, which is what shows "Coming Soon". Do not choose
    *Specific*: it needs a release-ready binary, and there is no release build while the blocker
    at the top of this file stands. Any choice other than *Specific* means a second review later,
    once a real date is set.
  - Expiry: no.
- **D. Pages → Product home.**
  - *Carousel*: `carousel/00-trailer.mp4` first, then `carousel/01`–`05`.
  - *About*: *About this game* above.
  - *Social preview*: `social_preview.jpg`.
- **E. Localization** is optional. Epic will translate the text for free on request.
- **F. Release Management.**
  - In *Dev*, choose *Push To Stage*.
  - Tick *Product configuration*, *Product home*, *Regions and ratings* and the offer.
  - Tick nothing under *Artifact data*: a Coming Soon page has no build.
  - In *Stage*, choose *Submit for Review*.
- **G. After approval.**
  - In *Stage*, choose *Push To Live* with the same entities.
  - The page is public from this moment.

## What this cannot produce, said plainly

- **A trailer filmed in a headset, or with the game's sound effects.** The trailer above is the
  desktop third-person camera, scored with the game's music only (see Trailer).
- **System requirements.** Nothing has been measured on real Windows hardware. What is known:
  the build is Electron (Windows 10 or later, 64-bit) and needs a WebGL 2 GPU. Minimum and
  recommended specs come from a test pass on real PCs (`ROADMAP.md`), not from this file.
- **The accounts.** A Steamworks partner account and the $100 Steam Direct fee per app
  (recoupable once the game earns $1,000), an Epic developer account and Epic's $100 submission
  fee per game, tax and bank forms, a privacy policy at a live URL, and an IARC rating (required
  by neither store for a page, recommended by Epic) are all account-holder actions.
- **A headset screenshot.** As on Meta, there is no headset here; the screenshots are the desktop
  third-person camera, which is what a Steam or Epic player sees without one.

## Things these stores require that already hold

- **Epic: achievements.** Epic requires its own achievements when a game has achievements on
  another PC store. This build integrates no store achievements (no Steamworks SDK); the game's
  own achievements live on its own server, so nothing is triggered. If Steam achievements are
  added, Epic's must be added in the same release.
- **Epic: crossplay.** Satisfied by one shared server — once there is one (the blocker above).
- **Both: no money in the game.** `LAUNCH_STORE` is empty and `validateCatalog` refuses any price
  in cents at boot, so neither store's payment rules have anything to apply to. If that ever
  changes, both require their own payment flow on their own build.
